7 Stocky Alternatives Compared (2026): Pricing, Migration, Forecasting

With Stocky shutting down for good on August 31, 2026, a wave of apps have positioned themselves as the replacement. They're not interchangeable — some are lightweight forecasting layers, some are full ERPs, and pricing ranges from roughly $30/month to several hundred. Here's what we could verify about each, priced and sourced as accurately as public information allows.

What to actually evaluate

Before the comparison table, the questions that matter more than any feature list:

  • Does it do forecasting, or just POs? Some tools are PO/receiving managers with no demand prediction; others lead with AI forecasting and treat POs as secondary.
  • Is pricing flat or usage-based? A flat monthly fee is predictable. Per-user or per-order pricing can balloon as you scale.
  • Does it have a Stocky migration path? Given the timing, a direct CSV importer for Stocky's PO and product export format saves real hours.
  • Is it built for retail/manufacturing, or for DTC e-commerce? MRPeasy and similar ERPs assume you're managing production and BOMs, not just reordering finished goods from suppliers — that's a very different job.

Prediko in more depth

Prediko has positioned itself aggressively as the Stocky replacement, going as far as publishing a dedicated Stocky-sunset migration page. Its pitch is AI demand planning (trained on a large cross-merchant SKU dataset), a 12-month PO calendar, and a "buying table" workflow for building POs against forecasted need. It supports raw materials and multi-location inventory, and lists a large number of WMS/3PL integrations. If your main pain point is forecast accuracy across a large catalog, this is worth a serious look. The tradeoff for evaluating it: exact current pricing wasn't consistently published across the sources we checked, so get a quote before assuming a number.

inFlow Inventory in more depth

inFlow is a mature, general-purpose inventory management platform, not a Shopify-native tool — it connects to Shopify via a dedicated app but its core is built to serve any sales channel. That makes it a stronger pick if you sell across Shopify, wholesale, and other channels and want one inventory backbone. Pricing tiers reported by third-party trackers put it well above the lightweight PO tools (three digits monthly), so it's better suited to a business that would use its warehouse/barcode features fully rather than just the PO piece.

MRPeasy in more depth

MRPeasy is an ERP for manufacturers — bill of materials, production scheduling, drag-and-drop rescheduling. Per-user pricing starts at $49/month and rises with tier, and it explicitly is not a lightweight Stocky substitute: if you're a manufacturer who happened to use Stocky for purchasing raw materials, MRPeasy's scope makes sense. If you're a retailer buying finished goods to resell, it's more system than you need.

The apps we couldn't fully verify

Being straightforward about this: PO Pilot, Stockwise, and Orderpoint appear in searches and directories with inconsistent or missing public pricing and feature detail as of this writing, and Restockr's most visible listing is a different category of app entirely (back-in-stock alerts, not purchasing). Rather than fabricate numbers for these, we're flagging that you should go direct to the vendor before including them in a shortlist. This is a fast-moving category post-Stocky-announcement — new and small entrants change positioning quickly.

FAQ

Is there a direct, one-to-one replacement for Stocky?

Not exactly — no single app has 100% feature parity, because Stocky combined POS-specific inventory features with forecasting and purchasing. Most merchants end up combining native Shopify Admin for basic transfers/adjustments with a dedicated app for forecasting and purchasing.

Which alternative is cheapest?

Among apps with confirmed public pricing, options starting around $29–49/month (Stockloop's paid tier, MRPeasy's per-user Starter tier) are the lowest verified entry points; several other named tools didn't have confirmed public pricing at the time of writing.

Do any of these import Stocky data directly?

Several vendors, including Prediko and Stockloop, specifically advertise Stocky migration support or a Stocky CSV importer, given the timing of the sunset. Confirm the exact import format each supports before your export window closes.

Should I just use Shopify's native purchase orders instead of a third-party app?

If your volume is low and you don't need forecasting, it can work — but be aware of a documented gap: Shopify's native PO tool doesn't reliably update product unit costs from received POs, which matters if you track margin closely.
