Reorder Point Formula for Shopify Stores: A Practical Guide With Examples

If you've ever run out of a bestseller because you reordered "when it felt low," a reorder point turns that gut feeling into a number. Here's the actual formula, a worked example, and where it gets more complicated than the textbook version.

The formula

The standard reorder point formula, consistent with Shopify's own published guidance:

Reorder Point (ROP) = (Average Daily Sales Velocity × Lead Time in Days) + Safety Stock

If you want the bare-minimum version without a safety buffer:

ROP = Average Daily Sales Velocity × Lead Time in Days

Most operators shouldn't use the bare-minimum version — it assumes your supplier is always on time and your sales are perfectly steady, which is rarely true.

Worked example

Using the numbers from Shopify's own published example:

  • Daily sales velocity: 5 units/day
  • Lead time: 35 days
  • Safety stock: 15 units
ROP = (5 × 35) + 15 = 190

When your stock on hand hits 190 units, it's time to place the reorder — not when it hits zero, and not "sometime before it hits zero."

Where each input actually comes from

Daily sales velocity. Use a trailing average, not a single day. A 30 or 60-day trailing average smooths out day-to-day noise; for seasonal products, you may need a longer window or a seasonally-adjusted figure, since a straight trailing average will underestimate demand heading into a peak and overestimate it heading out of one.

Lead time. This is supplier lead time — the days between placing a PO and having stock available to sell, not just "days until the box arrives." If you have to receive, inspect, and shelve inventory before it's sellable, include that. Track your actual historical lead times per supplier rather than using their quoted lead time; quoted and actual often diverge, especially overseas.

Safety stock. This is the part with no universal formula — Shopify's own guidance acknowledges it's business-specific, driven by how variable your demand is, how reliable your supplier's lead time actually is, and how much of a stockout you can tolerate. A common practical approach: look at your worst historical lead-time overrun and your highest demand spike over a comparable period, and size safety stock to survive both happening at once, scaled down by how likely you think that combination actually is for this specific SKU.

Reorder point vs. safety stock — they're not the same thing

It's easy to conflate these. Safety stock is a buffer quantity; reorder point is a trigger threshold that includes that buffer. Safety stock answers "how much slack do I need." Reorder point answers "at what stock level do I act." Get the distinction wrong and you'll either double-count your buffer or forget it entirely.

A common mistake: one ROP for your whole catalog

Sales velocity and lead time vary by SKU, sometimes drastically — a fast-moving core SKU from a local supplier has a completely different ROP profile than a slow-moving seasonal item from an overseas supplier with a 60-day lead time. Calculating one blanket reorder point across your catalog (or worse, using a single "reorder when stock hits 10 units" rule for everything) is the single most common way merchants both overstock slow movers and stock out on fast ones simultaneously.

Where this breaks down without tooling

The formula is simple. Applying it correctly across a real catalog, with real supplier variability, is not — which is the actual reason forecasting apps exist. Manually recalculating ROP per SKU as sales velocity shifts, lead times drift, and new products launch is a spreadsheet job that scales badly past a few dozen SKUs. This is also exactly the gap left by Stocky's shutdown: Shopify's native Admin has no reorder point automation at all as of this writing — you can create a manual purchase order, but nothing calculates when you should, or flags that a SKU has crossed its threshold.

What good tooling actually adds on top of the formula

  • Recalculating sales velocity continuously as new orders come in, rather than as a manual monthly exercise
  • Tracking actual (not quoted) lead time per supplier, per SKU, over time
  • Flagging SKUs that have crossed their reorder point before you go looking for them
  • Letting safety stock vary by SKU risk profile instead of being one flat number

FAQ

What's a good starting lead time if I don't know my actual supplier lead time?

Use the supplier's quoted lead time as a starting point, but start logging actual received dates against PO dates immediately — most operators find their real average is longer than quoted, and that gap is exactly what safety stock needs to cover.

How often should I recalculate reorder points?

Sales velocity shifts with trends, seasonality, and marketing pushes, so recalculating monthly at minimum is reasonable for a manual process; automated tools typically recalculate continuously as new order data comes in.

Does Shopify calculate reorder points natively?

No. Shopify's native Admin purchase order tools let you create and receive POs manually but don't calculate reorder points, flag low-stock SKUs against a threshold, or forecast demand — that functionality left with Stocky and currently requires a third-party app.

What if I sell a new product with no sales history?

There's no clean formula fix for this — use a comparable existing product's velocity as a proxy, order conservatively for the first cycle, and replace the estimate with real data as soon as you have a few weeks of actual sales.
