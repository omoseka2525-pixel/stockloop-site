Reorder Suggestions

How the reorder math works, and what to check first when a suggestion looks wrong.

How does Stockloop calculate a reorder suggestion?

Three inputs: (1) your sales velocity — units sold per day, averaged over a rolling window (default 30 days, configurable to 14, 60, or 90); (2) the supplier's lead time in days, from the Suppliers tab; (3) a safety-stock buffer, a number of extra days of cover you set per product or collection (default 7 days). The suggested reorder quantity covers velocity × (lead time + safety stock) minus what you already have on hand and on order. You can see the exact numbers behind any suggestion by clicking it.

Why did my velocity window jump around after a sale or stockout?

A short window (14 days) reacts fast to real demand shifts but can be skewed by a promo spike or a stockout that suppressed sales. Switch to a 60 or 90-day window for steadier products, or manually exclude a date range (Settings → Velocity → Exclude dates) if a specific promotion or outage shouldn't count.

A suggestion looks wrong — how do I check why?

Click the suggestion to open its breakdown: velocity used, window, lead time, safety stock, and current stock/on-order quantities. Most "wrong" suggestions trace to a missing or outdated supplier lead time, or a velocity window that includes an unusual spike or stockout. Fix the underlying input and the suggestion recalculates automatically — you don't need to edit the suggestion itself.

Can I override a suggested quantity?

Yes, always. Open the suggestion or the draft PO and edit the quantity directly before sending — overrides never get overwritten by recalculation once a PO is created. If you frequently override the same product the same way, consider adjusting its safety-stock buffer instead so future suggestions start closer to what you actually want.

What's a sensible safety-stock setting?

As a starting point: fast-moving, reliable-supplier products can use 3–7 days; slow-moving or unreliable-lead-time products (especially imported goods) often need 14–30 days. There's no universal number — it should reflect how much lead-time variability and demand volatility you actually see for that product.

Does Stockloop account for seasonality?

Not automatically in v1 — the velocity calculation is a rolling average, not a seasonal forecast. For seasonal products, shorten the velocity window as the season approaches, or manually adjust safety stock upward ahead of known peaks (e.g., before a holiday).

Why does a new product have no reorder suggestion?

New products need sales history before Stockloop can calculate a velocity. Until then, set a manual reorder point (Product → Reorder settings) so Stockloop can still suggest a quantity based on your estimate rather than waiting for data.

Can I set different lead times per supplier for the same product?

Yes — lead time is set at the supplier level and applied to whichever supplier is assigned as primary for that product. If a product has backup suppliers with different lead times, switching the primary supplier updates the suggestion accordingly.
