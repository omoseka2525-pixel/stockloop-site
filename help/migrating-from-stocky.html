Migrating from Stocky

Stocky shuts down August 31, 2026 — here's exactly how to bring your data into Stockloop before then.

Getting Started Migrating from Stocky Reorder Suggestions Purchase Orders Receiving Suppliers Billing & Plans Data & Privacy Troubleshooting
Stocky is shutting down August 31, 2026 — will Stockloop import my Stocky data?

Yes. Go to Settings → Import from Stocky and upload the CSV exports for suppliers, products, and purchase order history (export these from Stocky before it shuts down — Stocky does not let Shopify migrate this for you automatically). Stockloop reconciles the import against your live Shopify catalog and flags any SKU or supplier it can't match so you can fix it manually. Do this well before August 31 — once Stocky is gone, its data cannot be recovered from anywhere but your own exports.

Which Stocky exports do I need, and how do I get them?

From Stocky, export: Suppliers (CSV), Purchase Orders (CSV), and Reorder/Min-Max settings if you use them. Stocky's own export tools are the only source for this — Shopify does not retain it after shutdown. We recommend exporting everything by September 30, 2026 even if you're not ready to switch yet, simply so the data exists somewhere safe.

Will my Stocky reorder points and min/max settings carry over exactly?

Stockloop reads imported min/max values as a starting point, but it recalculates suggestions going forward using its own velocity-and-lead-time model rather than freezing your old static thresholds forever. This usually improves accuracy within a few weeks, but expect the first suggestions after migration to shift somewhat from what Stocky showed.

I can't export something from Stocky (e.g. old POs) — is it lost?

If a field genuinely isn't exportable from Stocky, it will not be recoverable after August 31, 2026 — this is a Stocky limitation, not a Stockloop one. Export what you can now (suppliers and PO history are the most commonly missed). If you're stuck on a specific export, message us and we'll walk you through Stocky's export screens directly.

Does Stockloop cost more than Stocky, which was free?

Stockloop is a paid app because it's independently built and maintained (Stocky was subsidized as part of Shopify POS Pro). Pricing is flat — Free, $29, $59, or $99/month depending on catalog size and locations — with no per-order or per-PO fees, so your cost doesn't scale with sales volume the way some alternatives' usage-based pricing does.
